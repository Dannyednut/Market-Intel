## CARD 1: The Dual-Rail Merchant Network — free national rails in, stablecoins out
- **Thesis**: Domestic instant-payment rails in emerging markets are collapsing toward zero price — BCEAO's PI-SPI is free with ~175 participants and Wave just joined [F8], and the Philippines' ~P1.50 fee cap made transfers effectively free and drove volumes +50% [F13], with South Africa opening national payments to non-banks mid-2027 [F9]. Cross-border legs, however, still cost ~6.36% traditional vs ~1% stablecoin [F14], and Nigeria — ~60% of SSA stablecoin inflows — has formalized crypto with stablecoins entering PSV 2028 [C10, E14]. The construct exploits that split: collect domestically on free rails where the volume is, settle cross-border on stablecoins where the margin is.
- **Construct**: A JV between (a) an existing **licensed e-money institution in one UEMOA state** — reserving direct **PI-SPI participation** without a new licence round (Wave's route [F8]) — and (b) a **stablecoin liquidity desk partner** (Kora "One Rail"-class [F7] or Yellow Card-class [E14]). WHO does WHAT: the e-money JV holds the rail slot and regulatory interface; the orchestration entity sells a merchant collections API on PI-SPI at **zero MDR** (the Philippines lesson: free transfers buy volume, don't tax it [F13]); the liquidity desk provides two-way USDT/USDC pricing on XOF/NGN/GHS legs; local bank partners leg out fiat. **Revenue engine**: 60–100bps blended FX spread on converted cross-border volume (capturing ~20% of the ~5.4pt traditional-vs-stablecoin gap [F14]), flat SaaS of $10–25/mo per active merchant, and settlement float income where permitted. Monetization applies only to the ~20–30% of merchants with cross-border flows — the import-trade wedge (the dLocal×WU Cameroon→Nigeria corridor profile [F12]). **Cost engine**: compliance in one corridor at a time, two integration teams (bank-side + desk-side), intraday settlement working capital; distribution rented via rev-share with existing acquirers/POS players. **Dependencies**: PI-SPI slot via the e-money JV [F8]; CBN posture for the Nigeria leg [C10]; regulated custody (BNY/Citi-class infrastructure now exists [C8]); banking partner signing off on FX legality.
- **Moat**: Free-rail participation slots are scarce and slow to replicate (licence + scheme accreditation cycles in years); two-sided merchant flow prices better at market-makers than one-sided remittance flow — spreads improve with scale; regulator-facing transaction reporting becomes a resellable service. **Erosion**: Circle–Tazapay scaling B2B stablecoin settlement into the same corridors [F2]; Wave verticalizing from PI-SPI membership into merchant FX itself [F8]; the 21-bank consortium stablecoin recapturing the settlement leg at sub-50bps [C4]; regulators exporting the Philippines fee-cap logic to cross-border spreads [F13].
- **First 3 moves (90 days)**: (1) Sign the e-money JV and file for PI-SPI direct participation in Côte d'Ivoire or Senegal citing the Wave precedent [F8]; register the merchant orchestration entity in parallel. (2) Contract one liquidity desk partner for two-way USDT/USDC pricing on XOF/NGN/GHS with a $5M/mo pilot volume commitment [F7, E14]. (3) Onboard 3 design-partner merchant groups with cross-border supply chains; run a shadow-ledger bake-off — PI-SPI collections vs current POS, stablecoin settlement vs current bureau rates — publish the delta as the sales asset.
- **Kill criteria**: BCEAO reprices PI-SPI for non-banks or restricts merchant use-cases; target-corridor stablecoin spreads compress below 150bps roundtrip for two consecutive quarters; Nigeria's PSV 2028 implementation effectively bans merchant stablecoin settlement [C10]; a consortium bank stablecoin offers the same merchants settlement below 50bps [C4].
- **Conflicts**: Circle–Tazapay ($400M, >$25B volume [F2]); dLocal×Western Union payout corridors [F12]; Wave [F8]; Kora One Rail (partner-or-competitor [F7]); 21-bank joint stablecoin (H1 2027 [C4]).
- **Capital intensity**: Asset-light-to-moderate (licences, integration, settlement working capital; no lending book).

## CARD 2: The Session-Bridge — market-neutral liquidity across 24/7 tokenized and session-based equities
- **Thesis**: Price for the same US share will soon exist around the clock on fragmented venues: the OKX×ICE JV has filed a 24/7 tokenized venue for 63 NMS symbols on permissioned AMMs under the SEC Innovation Exemption [E2, E1]; NYSE/Nasdaq are extending 23h→24h and Robinhood already runs 24h weekends [E13]; tokenized stocks exceed $2.9B and offshore TradFi perps already turn ~$433B/month [E3, E7]. Nobody is the neutral reference maker across these venues. The construct is the market-neutral provider that quotes tokenized NMS names during closed US sessions and hedges at the open — the carry trade of time zones.
- **Construct**: A broker-dealer-affiliated market-making firm that (1) files for **Innovation Exemption participant status** (permissioned-AMM LP) while the window is young and the symbol set is only 63 [E1, E2]; (2) takes maker access on all tokenized venues (OKX×ICE, Kraken-ecosystem with Nasdaq's backing [E3], LSE 24 for the UK leg [E4]); (3) hedges via index futures, the 23h/24h sessions as they activate [E13], and matched offshore perps where TradFi-perp liquidity already sits [E7]. WHO does WHAT: the firm quotes two-way pinned to fair value = last close adjusted for futures drift, related-listing moves (ADRs/UK listings [E4]), and offshore perp funding; a routing/smart-order API is sold to brokers and wallets. **Revenue engine**: 15–35bps gross capture on off-hours volume (wider spreads, necessity-driven taker flow — settlement deadlines, EM retail), venue maker rebates, plus a white-label "24h equities" API (target 20–40% of revenue at scale, near-zero marginal cost — the wedge MetaMask-as-payments-company would buy [C14]). **Cost engine**: hedging/inventory capital (~$50–150M to support $1–3B notional/month), exchange connectivity, and the ops killer — a real-time corporate-actions engine (dividends, splits, halts) mapped across tokenized venues. **Dependencies**: Innovation Exemption surviving its 5-year window [E1]; venue LP partnerships; prime/clearing relationship; corporate-actions data feed.
- **Moat**: Only-neutral-party positioning — every venue needs an external reference price it can't produce itself; the corporate-actions engine is months of grinding ops to replicate; cross-venue netting makes hedging capital more efficient than any single-venue internalizer. **Erosion**: ICE/OKX internalizing market-making into the JV [E2]; Coinbase's fee cuts + licence stacking building the same desk [E11]; CME extending 24/7 derivatives toward single names [E12]; structural decay — NYSE/Nasdaq's own 24h extension shrinks the dislocation window [E13].
- **First 3 moves (90 days)**: (1) Form the BD affiliate and file for Innovation Exemption participant status — early participants shape permissioning templates while the symbol universe is 63 [E1, E2]. (2) Build the fair-value + corporate-actions engine against the 63-name universe; paper-trade closed-session spread series for 60 days; measure realized dislocation net of hedge cost. (3) Sign one liquidity-supply agreement with a tokenized venue seeking a professional LP [E2/E3] and one routing-API agreement with a broker/wallet needing 24h equities (MetaMask-class buyer [C14]).
- **Kill criteria**: SEC withdraws or materially narrows the Innovation Exemption in year one [E1]; realized closed-session dislocation nets <5bps after hedge costs over a rolling quarter; a single venue exceeds ~70% of tokenized volume and internalizes flow — pivot to being their LP or fold.
- **Conflicts**: OKX×ICE internalization [E2]; Coinbase [E11]; Binance's TradFi perp complex absorbing the same off-hours flow offshore [E7]; NYSE/Nasdaq 24h [E13]; CME 24/7 derivatives [E12].
- **Capital intensity**: Balance-sheet (hedging/inventory capital), not infrastructure-heavy.

## CARD 3: The Yield Router — non-issuer stablecoin cash-management shelf for EM treasuries
- **Thesis**: GENIUS bans issuer yield, so yield escapes to the wrapper layer — tokenized MMFs at ~$14.9B and tokenized deposits, with banks holding the moat [C2, C3] — while offshore apps already pay 10% APY on USDG in LatAm/Africa/S Asia [E8]. Meanwhile Fed cash yields 3.75–4.00% [C9], regulated-issuer coins are compounding (RLUSD +50% m/m [C1]), and Nigeria sits at 23% policy rates with ~60% of SSA stablecoin inflows [C10]. The construct: a distributor that **never issues a coin and never pays yield itself** — it routes idle payment stablecoins into regulated tokenized MMF/yield-token wrappers for EM fintech treasuries, exporters, and marketplaces, and takes placement economics. Compliant with the yield ban by construction.
- **Construct**: A non-US fund distributor + securities intermediary. WHO does WHAT: wrapper issuers supply the yield-bearing security (a retail-accessible USDY-class token — Ondo passed BUIDL [C5] — plus one institutional MMF token [C3]); the distributor builds swap/redemption infrastructure (payment stablecoin → wrapper share → back to stablecoin, T+0/T+1) and signs white-label distribution deals with EM wallets/exchanges (Yellow Card-class [E14]); MiCA-compliant EMI partners + BNY/Citi-class custody carry the EU leg [C7, C8]. Segmentation: fintech float, exporter/freelancer USD balances (Nigeria/Kenya/Philippines), wallets white-labeling the shelf. **Revenue engine**: 25–50bps placement/management fee on AUM plus 40–80bps FX spread on local-currency↔USD on/off-ramp turnover. Unit logic: $100M AUM at 35bps ≈ $350k/yr against ~$400k infra+compliance — breakeven ~$120M AUM per market cluster; FX legs carry the margin until AUM compounds. **Cost engine**: fund administration, custody integration [C8], per-jurisdiction securities counsel, local on/off-ramp partners. **Dependencies**: exclusive wrapper partnership [C5]; light licences (EMI/broker-dealer); GENIUS boundary staying at "issuer" [C2]; MiCA passporting [C7].
- **Moat**: Exclusive-ish distribution agreements in EM are slow to build; the legal posture itself ("we never pay yield — the fund does") is a position competitors must rebuild from scratch; redemption-liquidity management (honoring T+0 in stress) is an ops moat that shows up exactly when competitors blow up. **Erosion**: the bank consortium stablecoin bundling tokenized deposits with yield for its own clients [C4, C3]; a market-structure bill unstalling to permit yield at issuers/affiliates, collapsing differentiation [C2]; Coinbase and MetaMask building the shelf vertically [E11, C14].
- **First 3 moves (90 days)**: (1) Sign the wrapper partnership — one retail-accessible yield token (USDY-class [C5]) + one institutional MMF token — with exclusivity for two named EM regions. (2) White-label pilot with two EM wallets/exchanges [E14] targeting fintech and exporter treasuries; target $25M AUM in 90 days; prove T+0 redemption discipline with a live stress drill. (3) File distributor licences in Nigeria inside the PSV-2028 clarity window [C10] and one LatAm hub; stand up EU custody via BNY-class infrastructure [C8] for a later euro-corridor product [C7].
- **Kill criteria**: US market-structure legislation extends the yield ban to distribution/affiliates — the shelf becomes illegal rather than merely unattractive [C2]; tokenized-MMF yield compresses below local deposit alternatives minus 100bps in ≥2 flagship markets [C9, C10]; a consortium bank launches zero-fee instant swap between its stablecoin and tokenized deposits for the same segment [C4, C3].
- **Conflicts**: OKX Money's USDG 10% APY — the informal offshore version that could also poison the category's regulatory tail [E8]; 21-bank joint stablecoin + tokenized deposits [C4, C3]; Circle–Tazapay bundling yield-routing into B2B settlement [F2]; GENIUS/market-structure uncertainty itself [C2].
- **Capital intensity**: Asset-light (fees on AUM; pass-through redemption keeps principal risk minimal).

## CARD 4: Warehouse-to-IPO — the bank-anchored local-currency credit platform for African lending fintechs
- **Thesis**: Africa's proven winners are balance-sheet businesses — OPay grew revenue +136% with ~60% of it from lending [F3], and MNT-Halan IPO'd 11x oversubscribed on EGX [F4] — yet the equity ladder beneath them is broken: $2B deployed in H1'26 but three mega-deals took $1.3B, median seed is $1.2M, and deal count is the lowest since 2020 [F5]. Absa just became the first African bank LP in Lendable's $255M fintech credit fund [F6], and CBN's 23% rate makes local-currency lending spreads enormous [C10]. The construct: productize the Absa×Lendable precedent into a multi-bank, multi-tranche warehouse that buys lending fintechs' loan books in local currency.
- **Construct**: A credit platform (fund + SPV warehouses) with three stacked tranches: (a) **senior local-currency notes placed with African commercial banks** (the Absa precedent [F6]) at ~T-bill+250–300 with zero FX risk — matching their deposit base; (b) mezzanine from DFIs and Lendable-class credit funds; (c) equity/first-loss from the platform plus a 10–20% fintech skin-in-the-game covenant. WHO does WHAT: fintechs originate (OPay-class distribution), the platform purchases receivables weekly at vintage-priced discounts with real-time telemetry covenants; after 18–24 months of seasoning, pools are securitized or sold to banks/EGX-listed vehicles (the MNT-Halan local-capital-markets path [F4]). **Revenue engine**: 150–300bps management + structuring fees, performance fees at securitization, plus an underwriting-data subscription sold back to originators (the rating-agency wedge). Unit logic: $300M warehouse at ~2% all-in fees = $6M/yr platform revenue against ~$5–7M ops (credit team, telemetry, three jurisdictions) — breakeven ~$250M AUM. **Cost engine**: credit underwriters (the scarce input), bank funding placement, per-originator telemetry integration. **Dependencies**: bank senior-tranche buyers [F6]; DFI mezzanine; Egypt's 1-year consumer-fintech licence freeze [F10] actually helps — it makes existing originators' books the only growth path in that market.
- **Moat**: The local-currency senior tranche solves banks' deposit-matching problem — once an ALCO approves the structure, switching costs are institutional; proprietary vintage data across multiple originators makes the platform the only credible pricing benchmark for African consumer credit; Egypt's freeze [F10] channels all new lending growth through incumbent originators that you finance. **Erosion**: banks verticalizing by acquiring the originators outright; Lendable scaling with more Absa-type bank LPs [F6]; a sovereign/DFI program undercutting pricing; CBN rate cuts compressing the 23% spread thesis [C10].
- **First 3 moves (90 days)**: (1) Secure one anchor bank senior-tranche commitment — approach Absa directly; the second transaction is always cheaper than the first [F6] — sized ~$50M in NGN or XOF at T-bill+250. (2) Sign three origination fintechs with >24 months vintage data and $5M+/mo originations; embed telemetry; execute the first receivable purchase within 60 days. (3) Structure one seasoned-pool sale (~$10M) to an EGX-listed vehicle or bank balance sheet to establish the refi curve that makes the warehouse NPV-positive [F4].
- **Kill criteria**: No second or third bank signs a senior tranche within 12 months (single-LP dependence kills the thesis); vintage losses on the first two pools pierce the junior tranche (underwriting wrong at these rates [C10]); regulatory change in Nigeria/Egypt prohibiting non-bank loan purchase or capping yields below bank-senior economics [F10, C10].
- **Conflicts**: Lendable + Absa directly [F6]; OPay and MNT-Halan self-funding from IPO proceeds [F3, F4]; CBN rate path [C10]; banks building in-house lending on their own payment rails.
- **Capital intensity**: Balance-sheet-heavy (the fund itself); the GP/management entity is asset-light.

## CARD 5: The Reversal — converting wind-down agent networks into stablecoin cash nodes
- **Thesis**: Agent banking's payment economics are dying — Onafriq is winding down Baxi in Nigeria as A2A rails erode cash-in/cash-out and CBN rules tighten [F11] — but the physical network (agents, float, trust, KYC'd touchpoints) is precisely what stablecoin off-ramps lack, in the market holding ~60% of SSA stablecoin inflows with crypto newly formalized [C10, E14]. Remittance legs still cost ~6.36% traditional vs ~1% stablecoin [F14] and payout corridors are being built (dLocal×WU [F12]) — payouts end in cash. Buy the network at wind-down multiples; sell the last mile.
- **Construct**: A special-situations holdco that acquires distressed agent networks (Baxi-class) at ~0.5–1.5x replacement cost (agent acquisition runs $50–150/agent; a 10k-agent network = $0.5–1.5M organic build). WHO does WHAT: a licensed lead (Nigeria PSV/e-money licence, positioned ahead of the PSV 2028 window [C10]) sub-agents the network for four functions: cash-in (local currency → USDT credited), cash-out, legacy bill-pay, and remittance payout points for dLocal×WU-class partners [F12]. **Revenue engine**: 30–60bps commission on on/off-ramp volume paid by exchange and B2B-rail offtakers (Yellow Card-class [E14], Kora-class [F7], Circle–Tazapay-class [F2]) who otherwise spend 200–400bps building their own; payout fees on remittance corridors [F12]; legacy bill-pay/airtime margin covering 40–60% of agent fixed costs, making incremental stablecoin revenue nearly pure. Unit logic: 10,000 active agents × $300/mo float velocity × 45bps take ≈ $135k/mo network revenue — the scale lever is float velocity, not agent count. **Cost engine**: agent commissions (60–70% of revenue), per-agent float caps and liquidity management, and compliance monitoring as the core cost (regulatory + A2A erosion is why Baxi died [F11] — treat compliance as product). **Dependencies**: the licensed lead [C10]; exchange/B2B offtake agreements with minimum-volume commitments [E14, F7]; float insurance; wind-down asset availability [F11].
- **Moat**: Physical presence is the last non-digital moat in payments — you cannot download an agent; multi-year exclusive liquidity contracts with exchanges; float-network effects (deep-float agents get more routing → deeper float). **Erosion**: the same A2A/free-rail force that killed Baxi eventually shrinks cash-out demand itself [F11, F8]; banks and MNO-style players entering off-ramps on their own agent bases; regulator redrawing agent rules mid-flight.
- **First 3 moves (90 days)**: (1) Approach Onafriq for the Baxi package in wind-down — network, agent contracts, float infrastructure — at distress terms; diligence agent-level P&L for 90-day retention risk [F11]. (2) Sign one anchor offtake (Yellow Card- or Kora-class [E14, F7]) with a minimum-volume commitment; convert a 500-agent pilot in 60 days under the licensed lead. (3) Open a formal CBN engagement to align the licence posture with PSV 2028 [C10] — be in the room writing agent-stablecoin rules rather than litigating them after [E14].
- **Kill criteria**: Agent retention below 60% at 6 months post-acquisition (trust doesn't transfer — the asset evaporates); off-ramp take rates compress below 20bps (no spread over agent commissions); CBN rules prohibit non-bank stablecoin cash conversion [C10]; cash-out demand declines >15% YoY in pilot corridors on A2A/free-rail adoption [F8].
- **Conflicts**: Onafriq retrenching into stablecoin itself rather than selling [F11]; dLocal×Western Union building proprietary payout networks [F12]; Yellow Card's $40M raise — well-capitalized enough to buy networks directly [E14]; bank-led agent estates; Wave's PI-SPI expansion reducing cash-out demand in UEMOA [F8].
- **Capital intensity**: Moderate — acquisition capital for the buyout plus float working capital; no lending book.
