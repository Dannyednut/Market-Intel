## 5. NEW STRUCTURAL PROBLEMS

Three candidates clear the evidence bar this cycle. One related candidate is declined.

**P10. Consent-institutionalization squeeze: issuer consent is being institutionalized into transfer-agent coalitions and possible rule-based opt-in, converting a commercial whitespace into a compliance utility with thin margins for third parties.**
Refs: [C.1][C.5][N6][W#4]
MECHANISM: P8 established consent as the scarce input; this cycle shows its **administration** being claimed by vertically integrated incumbents. Bullish (NYSE-listed) + Equiniti launched the IST Coalition (Sept 24) with Alpaca, Apex Fintech, DriveWealth — grounded in Bullish's **$4.2B Equiniti acquisition (closing Jan 2027)** to build "the world's leading transfer agent for tokenized securities"; Securitize runs issuer-sponsored security entitlements across NYSE and OKXICE distribution; Nasdaq bakes consent into product design ("issuer-first"). Simultaneously SIFMA asks the SEC for affirmative opt-in consent plus a universal opt-out. Either way — coalition-owned or rule-mandated — a standalone third-party consent gateway loses pricing power: margins compress toward transfer-agent/compliance-utility economics, and signed content (the asset) is increasingly locked by the coalitions that also hold the issuer relationships.
FIRST-ORDER EVIDENCE: 16 objectors / 17-of-63 symbols removed pre-launch [W#4][C.1]; content bps unpriced at every venue (zero posted rates) [C.5]; first "prices" forming structurally inside platform economics, not as rates [C.5]; 10+ law-firm alerts formalize the advisory layer [C.5]; SIFMA opt-in ask + Atkins/Uyeda rulemaking signal [C.1]; Perpetuals PDC's filing makes signed content scarce across two applicants [N6]; Card 6 at 80% of its >20-objection kill threshold [W#4].
SECOND-ORDER IMPLICATIONS: (i) Card 6's 12–18-month time-box now competes against a better-capitalized rival with a Jan 2027 closing transfer agent — the whitespace either consolidates (sign issuers before Equiniti closes) or dies as a standalone; (ii) if universal opt-out lands, the licensable universe shrinks to issuer-sponsored names only — the consent market splits into "sponsored" (issuers monetizing) and "blocked" (universal opt-outs), no middle; (iii) value migrates to whoever already holds issuer relationships at scale (transfer agents, incumbent exchange groups), not to intermediaries renting access.
EXPOSED: third-party consent gateways (Card 6); venues without issuer relationships; tokenizers relying on the opt-out default (OKXICE's current design).
BENEFICIARIES: Bullish–Equiniti stack; Securitize; incumbent exchange groups; transfer agents as a class.
SEVERITY: **Medium** market-wide; **High** for Card 6 specifically.
TRAJECTORY (6–12mo): Oct 27 NYSE IST event = first consolidation checkpoint; SEC rulemaking posture = the structural fork.
FALSIFIERS: SIFMA ask ignored + IST Coalition signs no issuers by Q1'27 + content bps emerge as posted third-party rates; or objections fall to ≤5 (consent not scarce after all).

**P11. Yield-ban perimeter globalization: the ban on paying users yield on stablecoin balances is hardening simultaneously in the US (interpretive), the EU (MiCA widening push), and EM (Kenya's ban) — closing the jurisdictional-arbitrage layer that exchange-paid yield and EM dollar-savings models relied on.**
Refs: [W#1][W#2][B.4][N14][D.2#4]
MECHANISM: Cycle-002's I1 found the enforcement gap (USDG yield sold offshore under MAS oversight, untouched). This cycle the gap's edges moved inward from three directions at once. **US:** CLARITY failed Senate cloture 49–50 (markup ~May 2027), killing the legislative path to extend the ban to distributors — which paradoxically leaves extension to Treasury/OCC interpretation (C002 prior 40–50%) [W#1]. **EU:** ECB/EU central banks pushing to widen MiCA's yield ban to lending/staking (Sept 22–Oct 2) [W#1]. **EM:** Kenya's VASP rulebook **bans paying interest on stablecoins from November 2026** [B.4][N14]; Ghana's December regime is the next candidate to embed analog terms [W#7]. Meanwhile the subsidized products keep spreading venue-to-venue (Gate's money app, Oct 7) [W#2][D.2#4] — subsidy competition peaking exactly as the perimeter hardens.
FIRST-ORDER EVIDENCE: three independent jurisdictions moving the same direction in one cycle [W#1][B.4]; zero regulator touch on OKX 10% APY (adjacent Texas transmission fine only) [W#2]; **no issuer-paid distribution yield to EM users anywhere**; Circle retains ~95% of reserve interest [B.4].
SECOND-ORDER IMPLICATIONS: (i) EM dollar-savings front ends (whitespace #8 wait-state) face a shrinking set of legal wrappers — the "licensed local wrapper" re-entry condition must clear a moving perimeter; (ii) yield-attach models (C3 rider) lose the offshore arbitrage leg progressively — surviving yield expression migrates into bank products (P1's beneficiaries again) and securities wrappers (tokenized T-bills/credit, P4), not into banned retail APY; (iii) enforcement asymmetry — EM bans bite fastest (Kenya November), US/EU interpretive paths drag — creating a patchwork where the same product is legal in one market and banned in an adjacent one, raising structuring costs and concentrating subsidized yield in ever-fewer offshore venues.
EXPOSED: EM dollar-savings front ends; yield routers (C3); exchange-paid APY programs (OKX/Gate-class); issuer EM distribution partnerships.
BENEFICIARIES: banks (deposit products reabsorb savings demand); licensed EM custodians offering non-interest wrappers; tokenized T-bill/credit issuers where securities law permits.
SEVERITY: **Medium-high** for the yield-attach/EM-savings design space; low market-wide.
TRAJECTORY (6–18mo): Kenya bites November; Ghana regime December; ECB push either enters MiCA review text or stalls; OCC rule (Oct 31) decides whether the US joins via interpretation.
FALSIFIERS: MiCA widening explicitly rejected in review; Kenya's ban unenforced or withdrawn; OKX APY still live and expanding untouched at Mar 31 2027 with no new jurisdiction bans → the gap is durable and P11 demotes to a watch item.

**P12. Float-yield decay: the EM local-currency float-yield leg — underwritten as a durable moat — is macro-rented, not owned, and the rent is being cut (NGN 15.90% and easing; MPR 23% and falling).**
Refs: [B.4][B.5][N14][N15]
MECHANISM: The EM off-ramp model's underwriting assumed local-currency prefunding float earns near-policy yields (C002 anchor: NGN 16.84% ≈ +46bps per 10-day cycle). That yield is set by EM central banks fighting inflation — and the fight is being called off. Nigeria's MPC **cut 350bps to 23.0%** with further cuts expected (IMF endorsing, warning against haste); the 364-day T-bill stop is **15.90%** (−95bps vs the stale C002 vintage; DMO rejected higher-yield bids across ₦763bn); Ghana (~9–10%, auction misses) and Kenya (9.04%) no longer anchor [B.4]. The yield is harvested by whoever holds customer funds — Cameroon's Orange Money/MTN MoMo booked **CFA21.5B** on safeguarded float [N14] — so operators cannot retake it by integration; they can only lose it as rates fall. Symmetry with P9: the payout margin is a revocable licence granted by rail owners; the float margin is a rented tailwind granted by central banks.
FIRST-ORDER EVIDENCE: NGN 364d 15.90% (Oct 14 auction) [B.4]; MPR 23% (−350bps, Sept MPC) [B.4]; Ghana 364d ~9–10% with a Gh¢1.53bn auction miss [B.4]; Kenya 9.04% [B.4]; Card 1's float add-on compresses ~46→~40bps per cycle and shrinks per cut [B.5].
SECOND-ORDER IMPLICATIONS: (i) moat re-anchoring — Ghana weighting now rests on the FX wedge (4.8–5.1%), not yield; **Nigeria is the only high-yield float leg left**, which concentrates single-country exposure exactly as JPMorgan prepares an onshore Nigerian entry [B.5][N1]; (ii) model staleness — any construct underwritten at 16.84% (or MPR 26.5%) is overstated; re-underwrite at 15.90% on a declining path; (iii) the durable-margin triad (wedge + exclusivity + float yield) collapses toward two legs, raising reliance on the policy-artifact wedge — the same regulator cutting rates is the one that could mandate spread disclosure (P2), so the two clocks compound.
EXPOSED: EM off-ramp operators with NGN prefunding float; Card 1's float-yield pool; corridor models that priced float yield as a permanent moat.
BENEFICIARIES: the real economy (intended); MNOs and banks, who still harvest the float at shrinking yields; no one in the off-ramp tier.
SEVERITY: **Medium** (margin compression, not model death), rising with each cut.
TRAJECTORY (6–18mo): follows Nigeria's easing path; each MPC cut trims the float add-on roughly proportionally.
FALSIFIERS: inflation re-acceleration reverses the cycle (MPR back toward 26%+ or T-bill stops >16.5%) → decay pauses/reverses; or operators contract float-yield-sharing with custodians (converts rented yield into contracted yield).

**Declined: "three-way payout-leg attack" as a standalone problem.** Mechanism, exposed parties, and beneficiaries are P9's (as C002 declined float-vs-flow into P1); formalized instead as P9's reframe + chain I7. Fed-hike risk is uncertainty U8, not a structural problem.
