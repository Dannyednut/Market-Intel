Board read in one line: capital is flooding into issuer/venue plays [C11] while the scarce assets — EM licences, last-mile payout networks, and the yield-routing seam — sit undervalued, and the stablecoin float is undergoing its first live macro stress test [C1][C9].

## WEAK SIGNALS

**1. Synthetic beat tokenized: TradFi perps are eating the 24/7-equities thesis.**
Signal: offshore TradFi perp volume hit ~$433B in Aug (~15x Jan) [E7], while total tokenized stock value is ~$2.9B (+7.4% m/m) [E3] — the real 24/7 equities flow went synthetic, not tokenized, by a ~150x margin.
Why it matters: tokenization headlines [E1][E2][E4] are watching the wrong rail. Perp order flow means price discovery, funding markets, and hedging demand accrue to venues that never touched the underlying — this decides who wins onshore when single-stock 24/5 perps clear the SEC/CFTC [E11].
Refs: [E7][E3][E11][E12].
Confirm: US single-stock perp approvals advancing; perp notional crossing tokenized-cash volumes by another order; CFTC enforcement posture on offshore equities perps.

**2. The stablecoin contraction looks rate-driven, not risk-off — the composition proves it.**
Signal: supply fell $321B → $300.9B [C1] exactly as the Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00% [C9]. But Tether shrank while RLUSD grew +50% m/m [C1]. Pure risk-off shrinks everything; a split contraction = holders routing yield (MMFs, tokenized deposits, APY platforms) [C3][E8].
Why it matters: this is the first live measurement of float elasticity. If cyclical, rate cuts re-inflate the float — and the routing infrastructure built during contraction keeps the float afterwards.
Refs: [C1][C9][C3][E8].
Confirm: supply re-accelerating on a Fed pause; tokenized Treasury AUM [C5] growing faster than total float.

**3. African fintech funding is flipping from equity to bank balance sheets.**
Signal: equity is barbelled and early-stage is hollowing (median seed $1.2M, deal count lowest since 2020) [F5], while Absa became the first African bank LP in an oversubscribed $255M fintech credit fund [F6] and EGX retail oversubscribed a lending IPO 11x [F4].
Why it matters: banks are re-entering fintech through the credit book, not the cap table — the 2019–21 "banks vs fintechs" frame is dead. Debt replaces venture as the sector's capital structure within ~4 quarters.
Refs: [F5][F6][F4].
Confirm: a second African bank LP appearing in fintech credit vehicles; lender IPOs pricing above private marks; equity rounds for consumer lenders drying further.

**4. Central banks are opening a data war on stablecoin remittance economics.**
Signal: vendors claim ~1% stablecoin remittance cost vs 6.36% traditional; Bank of Italy publicly disputes the end-to-end figure [F14].
Why it matters: the Philippines shows what happens when a regulator acts on corridor-cost data — it capped fees and volumes still grew +50% [F13]. Fee-cap and licensing decisions across EM will be made on contested measurements; whoever owns verified end-to-end data shapes the policy.
Refs: [F14][F13][F12].
Confirm: BIS/World Bank methodology papers on corridor cost attestation; a second G20 central bank publishing a stablecoin-cost rebuttal.

**5. Nigeria is running the sector's first double-squeeze stress test.**
Signal: CBN cut 350bp to 23% [C10] while free A2A erodes agent/fee income (Onafriq winding down Baxi, explicitly citing A2A erosion) [F11] — against a business model where lending is ~60% of revenue [F3] — while simultaneously opening stablecoin doors (PSV 2028: 68 mentions; crypto formalized) [C10][E14].
Why it matters: fee income and lending spread compress together, and the escape lane (stablecoin/FX services) is being opened by the same regulator. OPay's IPO filings [F3] will disclose this in public for the first time.
Refs: [C10][F11][F3][E14][F8].
Confirm: OPay take-rate and net-interest margin disclosure at IPO; further agent-network wind-downs; CBN guidance on lending-rate floors.

**6. The bank stack is being pre-positioned under the GENIUS headline noise.**
Signal: OCC permits tokenized reserves; tokenized deposits + MMFs at ~$14.9B [C3]; BNY custody live in EU, Citi packaging "Custody+" on $34.5T AUC [C8]; 21 banks committed to a joint USD stablecoin company for H1 2027 [C4].
Why it matters: everyone is watching crypto issuers; the bank side is quietly solving the same yield problem with a moat (deposits) that non-banks can't replicate [C3]. H1 2027 launch means distribution decisions are being made now.
Refs: [C3][C4][C8][C9].
Confirm: consortium charter/member list published; tokenized-deposit pilot volumes disclosed; more G-SIB custody launches.

## STRATEGIC EDGES

**1. The platform-yield seam (regulatory).** GENIUS bans issuer-paid yield [C2], but platform-paid rewards (OKX 10% APY on USDG [E8]) and affiliated-MMF routing [C3] remain lawful while the Senate bill is stalled over exactly this [C2]. Held by: custody platforms with affiliated asset managers — Coinbase, OKX, MetaMask [C14], and banks via tokenized deposits [C3]. Compounds: ~$11–12B/yr of float yield is being routed [C1][C9]; every quarter of routing builds switching costs and float custody that persist after the seam closes — the habit outlives the loophole.

**2. EM licence-stack scarcity vs capital flooding the wrong layer (structural).** Egypt froze licences [F10]; Nigeria's licensed cohort is brand new [E14][C10]; SA's window opens mid-2027 [F9]; MiCA hard-gates the EU [C7] — while 45% of all fintech VC piles into digital assets concentrated in payments/exchange/brokerage [C11][C12] and African early-stage funding collapses [F5]. Held by: licensed EM operators (OPay [F3], Wave [F8], dLocal [F12], Yellow Card [E14]) and strategic buyers (Circle [F2], Kraken [E9]). Compounds: distressed licence/asset supply (funding drought [F5]) meets rising demand for licensed distribution (H1 2027 bank launch [C4], SA opening [F9]) — a widening buy-side spread for whoever holds the licences.

**3. Synthetic-flow information advantage (structural).** The market already voted — $433B/month of offshore TradFi perps [E7] vs $2.9B tokenized stocks [E3]. Perp order-flow owners see price discovery, funding rates, and hedging demand before tokenized/regulated venues do. Held by: Binance [E7], OKX [E2][E8], CME (24/7 live, record ADV [E12]), Coinbase (filings in flight [E11]). Compounds: flow → market-maker density → liquidity moat, plus proprietary order-flow data that wins onshore licence filings [E11] and informs where to list the 63-symbol tokenized venues [E2] — the perps book becomes the R&D lab for the regulated product.
